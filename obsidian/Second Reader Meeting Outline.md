### Basic Frame
Background
- Fundamental timescale of planet formation
- Measured from stellar age
- 2 pathways: core accretion (~Myr), or gravitational instability (~$10^3$ yr)
- Core accretion: dust grains -> pebbles -> planetesimals -> planet. Once it is heavy enough, it pulls in gas from the disk and eventually becomes a gas giant.
- Gravitational instability: a dense region in the disk is gravitationally unstable -> it quickly collapses into a planet.
- It was thought that disks just formed the planets and that was it. But, late in-fall replenishes the disk, so more planets could form much later than expected

Actual findings
- What's found is that actually material can fall into the disk much later on in the timeline. This replenished disk can make a new planet much later than what our estimates allow us to find; thus the planet and star ages might differ several million years
- Therefore 2 pathways: primordial (similar age with star), replenished (Myr)
- That's where our current models break down; my project will be to reverse engineer this cooling curve fitting method, and using mass measurements taken with Kepler's laws, along with the brightness, to find the age of the planets.

We look at brightness and age to get mass. We wanna reverse this




Definitions:
- **Accretion**: process by which matter accumulates onto a more massive body, usually through gravitational forces -> cold start; planets start with low entropy
- **Gravitational Instability**: different process by which giant planets form directly from the collapse of gravitationally unstable regions in the disk -> hot start
