**Project CGI-4: From masses to ages.**
When directly observing extrasolar planets one uses their measured brightness in near-
infrared bands, the stellar ages as well as planet formation models to estimate their masses.
This is necessary because typically directly imaged planets are located at large separations
from their host stars, so that any orbital motion is slow. The stellar ages are in this case a
stand-in for the actual age of the planet, i.e. when it started to form. These ages are not only
somewhat uncertain, but it is indeed not clear what the timescale of planet formation is. Some
models predict this to be done within a few thousand years, while other require several millions
of years.

However, within the past decade we have now observed several of these direct imaging
planets for a long enough baseline to get some estimate of their masses based on Kepler’s
laws. We then can reverse the process and use the mass estimate to instead estimate the
age of the planet. From there we may be able to get a more fundamental understanding of the
typical timescale of planet formation when comparing the planetary age with the age of the
young star.
